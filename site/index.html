Plan the day. Then re-plan it when the day happens.

Tasks, habits, focus timers and a diary you can speak, in one store on your own devices. Connect an AI assistant and it reads what actually happened, moves what you missed, and has tomorrow ready before you wake.

One store, five ways in

Everything lives in one small database on each device. Whichever way you reach it, you see the same day.

  • Terminal

    myvault add, myvault today, myvault done. Every command also speaks JSON.

  • Desktop

    A Linux app with the full picture, sharing the terminal's data.

  • Phone

    An Android app, with home-screen widgets for today, habits, focus and a quick diary note.

  • Top bar

    A GNOME widget: the task on now, the focus countdown, a tick from the menu.

  • Assistant

    Claude Code or Codex can read and change it all, through tools that ship with it.

What it does

A day you can see at a glance

Tasks and habits sit on one timeline. The card on top always shows the task you should be on right now, with a ring for how much of the day is done. The grid above it fills in as the weeks go by.

  • Tasks take a date, a time slot, a list, a tag and notes. All optional.
  • Timed tasks can appear in your phone's calendar, and moving the event moves the task.
  • The Plan page shows the next seven days and folds the rest away.

Reminders you answer, not swipe away

When a task starts and the phone is locked, the reminder takes the whole screen: what it is, until when, and your notes. One tap on Doing it ticks it. Snooze brings it back in ten minutes.

  • While you are using the phone it is a banner with the same two buttons.
  • It follows the ringer: sound, vibration, or nothing on silent.
  • On the laptop the top-bar widget rings instead.

Habits that can have steps

Some habits are one tick. Others are a day's worth of small ones: three meals, three litres of water. Name them Water 3 L / 1 L down and each step reminds you at its own time, while the habit counts as done only when every step is.

  • Streaks, and how many of the last thirty days you kept each one.
  • Habits with a time sit in the day's timeline beside your tasks.

A focus timer that knows the task

Start 15, 25 or 50 minutes on a task from the app, a widget, the top bar or the terminal. The countdown shows everywhere, and the minutes are logged against the day.

Screen time, on both devices

Which apps, for how long, and at what hour, for the laptop and the phone together. The same app on both is one line. Ask your assistant where Tuesday afternoon went and it can tell you.

  • The phone reads Android's own record. The laptop notes the focused window.
  • Locked or idle time is not counted.

A diary you can just say

Log anything, any time: when you woke, an idea, a decision, how a task went. Type it, or tap the mic and say it. Every entry carries the time you logged it and can be attached to a task or a habit.

  • Speech is written down on the device by an open-source Whisper model. No account, no connection, and nothing you say is sent anywhere.
  • Pick the model once: any single language, or one tuned for mixed Hindi and English that writes in Roman letters.
  • Today stays open. Each day that is over folds into one line you can open again.
  • It works on the phone and on the Linux desktop.

An assistant that plans with you

The program contains no AI and needs no key. It gives your own assistant, Claude Code or Codex, the tools to read and change your day, and two skills that tell it how to do the bigger jobs.

For example

I woke at ten and skipped the run. Re-plan my day.

Done. I started from now and kept your order:

10:45–12:45  Write the methods section
13:30–15:00  Read two papers on graph sampling
16:00–16:30  Groceries for the week
17:00–18:00  Guitar: practise the new piece

The run moved to tomorrow at 07:00. I did not squeeze it in. Must-dos: the methods section, the two papers, groceries.

Ask in your own words

"What is on today?" "Move the review to Friday." "Where did my afternoon go?" The assistant uses the same store as the apps, so a change shows up on your phone a moment later.

Or let it run while you sleep

A scheduled session in the cloud can read the diary every night, move what was missed, time the new day around when you actually wake, and leave a one-line receipt. Your laptop can be off. You read the report over breakfast.

  1. Through the dayYou tick tasks and log the diary, by typing or speaking.
  2. At nightThe assistant reads every entry it has not handled yet.
  3. It re-plansMissed tasks move forward. A skipped course slides a day; it never stacks.
  4. In the morningThe new day is in your apps, with a short report of what moved and why.

It never deletes a task, never ticks one for you, and treats a diary entry as a note, not an order.

A memory of your own, filed for you

Optional. A vault is a folder of plain Markdown notes that you open in Obsidian. You keep logging the diary; the assistant puts each entry where it belongs. You never sort anything.

Where it went

  • raw/2026-10-13.mdall three, word for word, with their times
  • days/2026-10-13.mdthe day in short, linked to where each part went
  • Ideas.mdthe two-minute summaries
  • Decisions.mddropping the second experiment, and why
  • projects/Thesis/log.mdthe methods section needs a diagram
  • people/, learning/one note per person and per topic, added to over time
  • Me.mdyour goals, and how you like your day planned

  • The rules are yours

    A note in the vault says what goes where and how each note looks. Edit it and the filing follows.

  • It only ever adds

    Nothing you wrote is reworded or removed, and an entry is never filed twice.

  • It informs the plan

    Me.md is what the assistant reads before it times your day. Change your routine by editing a note.

It is yours

  • Works offline

    Each device holds a full copy. Nothing waits on a server.

  • Your own sync

    Devices sync through a free Supabase project that you create. Nobody else holds your data.

  • Speech stays put

    Voice is written down on the device. No audio leaves it.

  • Open source

    MIT licensed. No account with us, because there is no us: read it, change it, run it.

Quick start

  1. Install

    On 64-bit Linux, one line installs the todo command and the desktop app into your home folder.

    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Atharv-tw/vault-app/main/scripts/install.sh | sh -s -- --app

    For the phone, download the APK from the latest release. It needs Android 12 or newer.

  2. Answer three questions

    The installer ends with myvault init. Each answer is optional, and you can run it again to change one.

    Sync between devices?It walks you through a free Supabase project of your own.
    Connect Claude Code or Codex?It registers the tools and installs the two skills.
    Keep a vault?It creates the folder and its starter notes, in a private GitHub repo if you want one.

  3. Use it

    $ myvault add Revise unit 2 -d tomorrow -t 18:00-20:45
$ myvault habit add Read 20 min --time 22:15
$ myvault today

    Or say it to your assistant: "what is on today?", "I woke at ten, re-plan my day", "file my diary".

What it does not do

  • The ready-made programs are for 64-bit Linux and Android 12 or newer. There is no iPhone, Windows or Mac build. The desktop pieces are built and used on GNOME.
  • ChatGPT and other chat apps are not supported: they need a tool server on the internet, and this one runs on your machine.
  • Sync and the nightly planner take some setting up: a Supabase project of your own, and for the planner a Claude subscription that can run scheduled sessions.
  • Speech models are a one-time download of 100 to 375 MB, and words appear when you pause, not as you speak.
  • Reading the vault on a phone needs Obsidian and a way to sync the folder, which this does not provide.
  • I built it for my own days. Expect rough edges, and tell me about them.
